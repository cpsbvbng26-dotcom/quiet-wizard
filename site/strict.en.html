Principia: The Definitions and Laws of the Wizard

Principia Magi Occulti

This is a play. It takes the nine definitions and ten laws of the canon as its skeleton and rebuilds them in the form of a classical mathematics book.

Its strictness lies in how the words are put together. It does not mean that the content is scientifically correct. Nothing has been measured, and nothing has been tested.

This version was written by Claude at the operator's request. It is not the canon. The text of the canon stands unchanged, with an English rendering, on the Canon page.

Book I corresponds, item by item, to the nine definitions and ten laws of the canon.
Definition 10 and the two postulates were added in this version.
Book II moves the definitions and laws of Book I into formulas of many-sorted first-order predicate logic, and proves propositions about the system of laws and about shapes.
Book III sets out the dynamics of relationships between people as faces turned toward each other.
Book IV defines what magic is and proves its properties.
Book V shows why magic cannot be within knowledge: not because it does not exist, but because it must not. That conclusion rests on a norm set down as a postulate.
Each item that comes from the canon is stated here in this version's words, and the text of the canon is attached below it as the "Original", in Japanese and unchanged. The original of Definition 20 in Book V is the operator's intuition.
The Japanese version states each item in classical literary Japanese. This English version states them in plain English; it is a translation, and the Japanese page is the original of this version.
The arrangement follows the "Definitions" and the "Axioms, or Laws of Motion" of Newton's Mathematical Principles of Natural Philosophy, and the propositions and proofs of Euclid's Elements.

Book I. DefinitionsLIBER I · DEFINITIONES

Definition 1. Wizard. A wizard is one who acts using both their own negative and their own positive together, and produces results. The negative and the positive may be faces of the same element at different times.
Original自らの負と正の両方を用いて能動し、成果を出す者をいう。

Definition 2. Virgin. A virgin is one without sexual experience. Up to what time is settled where the word is used. The word names only a fact; it says nothing about a person's worth.
Original性経験のない者をいう。

Definition 3. Action. Action is everything a person does. Speaking out, recording, and reaching out are only part of it.
Original全ての能動をいう。発信、記録、働きかけに限らない。

Definition 4. Result. A result is what arises from action. Its form is set by the time and the circumstances.
Original能動によって生じるものをいう。その形は時と場合によって定まる。

Definition 5. Element. An element is what makes up a person. Every element has the shape of one of the five regular polyhedra.
Originalある者を構成するものをいう。要素は正多面体である。

Definition 6. Negative and positive. Negative and positive are the signs of the faces that an element turns according to the time and the circumstances. An element in itself is neither negative nor positive. The sign of a face, too, is settled only once a time and circumstances are given.
Original要素が時と場合によって向ける面をいう。要素そのものに負と正の別はない。

Definition 7. Accumulation. Accumulation is the negative piling up without fading. Its amount is measured by the number of times a negative has been shown.
Original負が消えずに溜まっていくことをいう。

Definition 8. Switch. The switch is the accumulated negative beginning to reveal the opposite of its face as positive. The time when it first appears is called the time of the switch.
Original蓄積した負が、逆の面を正として顕し始めることをいう。

Definition 9. Age thirty. Age thirty is a rule of thumb for the switch. A rule of thumb does not fix the time.
Original切り替わりの目安をいう。

Definition 10. Opposite. The opposite of a face is the face that lies point-symmetric to it about the centre of the polyhedron. A regular tetrahedron has no such face, so the vertex facing that face is taken as its opposite. This vertex, too, carries negative and positive in the same way as a face.
Originalなし（この版にて補ふ） (none; added in this version)

PostulatesPOSTULATA

Postulate 1. Time. Time moves only forward. What has happened is never undone.

Postulate 2. Worth. A person's worth does not depend on whether they are a wizard, or on whether they are a virgin.

Axioms, or LawsAXIOMATA, SIVE LEGES

Law 1. Establishment.LEX I · CONSTITUTIO Anyone who is still a virgin at thirty will at some time surely become a wizard. That time may come before thirty or after it.
Original三十歳まで童貞であれば、魔法使いになる。

Law 2. Many Faces.LEX II · MULTIPLICITAS Elements are not fixed. Depending on the time and the circumstances, they change the face they turn.
Original要素は固定されない。時と場合によって、向く面を変える。

Law 3. Conservation.LEX III · CONSERVATIO The negative does not perish; it piles up. A negative once shown is never withdrawn, and the accumulation never decreases.
Original負は消えない。蓄積する。

Law 4. Reversal.LEX IV · CONVERSIO When the accumulation of negative grows and reaches that person's threshold, the opposite of a face once shown as negative appears as positive.
Original負の蓄積が進めば、逆の面が正として顕れる。

Law 5. Rule of Thumb.LEX V · NORMA Thirty is only a rule of thumb. The switch can come before that age.
Original三十歳は目安にすぎない。切り替わりは年齢に先立つことがある。

Law 6. Return.LEX VI · RESTITUTIO A result, at the very moment it appears, returns to people's relationships without a ripple. So it cannot be seen from outside.
Original成果は、出た瞬間に社会的関係へ何事もなく還元される。ゆえに外からは見えない。

Law 7. Common Practice.LEX VII · COMMUNITAS Those who have become one all do this. That is, once someone has become a wizard, for as long as they live they never stop the actions that produce results.
Original成った者は、皆これを行っている。

Law 8. Quagmire.LEX VIII · PALUS Becoming one is a quagmire. After becoming one, the brain does not stop.
Original成れば泥沼である。脳は止まらない。

Law 9. Secrecy.LEX IX · ARCANUM Write down the specifics, and harm comes back to the one who wrote them. So the specifics are not written.
Original具体を記せば、記した者に害が返る。ゆえに具体は記さない。

Law 10. Long Life.LEX X · LONGAEVITAS To become one is to live long. Living long is hard. Its toil grows with the length of the life lived.
Original成ることは、長く生きることである。長く生きるのは大変である。
Note"Living long" here is a word of the play. It does not speak of the body's lifespan.

PropositionsPROPOSITIONES

Proposition 1. Whether someone is a wizard cannot be told from outside by their results.
ProofBy Law 6, results cannot be seen from outside. But Definition 1 requires producing results. So from outside, this condition cannot be confirmed through results. Q.E.D.

Proposition 2. Law 1 is a sufficient condition, not a necessary one. That is, someone who is not a virgin can also be a wizard.
ProofOnly Law 1 makes virginity a condition, and even it only describes what becomes of those who are virgins. Definition 1 does not include virginity. Indeed, in the model M₀′ of Book II, x is not a virgin, becomes a wizard at time 21, and breaks none of the laws (Book II, Proposition 10, whose proof uses no proposition of Book I). So a wizard who is not a virgin is possible. Q.E.D.

Proposition 3. These laws do not say that anything happens on the thirtieth birthday.
ProofLaw 1 says only that one will surely become one at some time, and allows that time to come before or after thirty. Law 5, too, calls thirty only a rule of thumb. By Definition 9, a rule of thumb does not fix the time. Q.E.D.

Proposition 4. These laws cannot be refuted by observing results from outside.
ProofTo show a case where Law 1 fails, one would have to show someone who is still a virgin at thirty and does not become a wizard. But by Law 6, results cannot be seen from outside. Results not being seen is no proof that someone has not become one. So no failing case can be shown by observing results from outside. Q.E.D.
NoteHere lies one of the reasons why these laws are not scientific laws. The proof in formulas is Proposition 11 of Book II.

Proposition 5. A wizard is not superior to other people.
ProofBy Postulate 2, a person's worth does not depend on whether they are a wizard. So no ranking of people can be drawn from being a wizard. Q.E.D.
Note"Not superior" here means not superior by reason of being a wizard.

Proposition 6. The opposite of a face of a regular tetrahedron is a vertex.
ProofPlace the centre of the regular tetrahedron at the origin; then its four vertices sum to zero. The centre of a face is the mean of the other three vertices, so if v is the vertex facing that face, the centre is −v/3. That is, each face centre is −1/3 times one of the four vertices. Reflecting it through the centre gives v/3. Since no vertex is point-symmetric to v, v/3 is the centre of no face. So a regular tetrahedron has no opposite faces, and by Definition 10 the opposite is the facing vertex. The other four regular polyhedra are point-symmetric about their centres, so every face of theirs has an opposite face. Q.E.D.

Proposition 7. For an element with n faces, there are 2ⁿ ways to assign negative or positive to each face.
ProofEach face has two options, negative and positive. With n faces, there are 2ⁿ assignments. A regular icosahedron has twenty faces, so for it there are 2²⁰, that is 1,048,576 ways. Q.E.D.

None of these is in the canon; all were written by Claude.

Proposition 8. Definition 6 and the ten laws are consistent.
ProofTake the following model M₀. P = {x}, C = {c}, L(x) = 100, Λ = 80, κ(x) = 1, and V(x, t) is always true. E(x) = {e}, where e is a regular hexahedron. g, f and f* are faces of e, with f* = ι(f) and g different from both. φ(e, t, c) is g for t < 20, f for t = 20, and f* for t > 20. For every face u, σ(u, t, c) is − at t = 20 and + otherwise. N = H. Act(x) = {a_s : 21 ≤ s ≤ 100}, τ(a_s) = s, Use(a_s) = {(e, 20, c), (e, s, c)}, R(a_s) = {r_s}. Rel(x) = {r_s : 21 ≤ s ≤ 100}, Obs = ∅, B is always true, Wr = ∅, and Toil(x, t) = t. Then H(x, t) is {(e, 20, c)} for t ≥ 20, A first reaches the threshold at time 20, Sw(x, t) holds for t ≥ 21, and W(x, t) holds for t ≥ 21. Substituting into D6 and into each of L1 to L10, every one comes out true. Q.E.D.

Proposition 10. There are two models that are equal in everything visible from outside but differ in whether someone is a wizard.
ProofLet M₀′ be M₀ with V(x, t) always false. Let M₀⁻ be M₀′ with the actions removed (Act(x) = ∅, Rel(x) = ∅). In both, V is false, so L1 is vacuously true. In M₀⁻, W is false, so L7 and L8 are vacuously true as well. The switch remains in both, so L4 and L5 are true. So both satisfy D6 and the ten laws. Obs is empty in both, so they are equal with respect to 𝒪 = {Obs}. Yet W(x, 100) is true in M₀′ and false in M₀⁻. Also, in M₀′, x becomes a wizard without being a virgin. Q.E.D.

Proposition 11. If the length of life is taken as not visible from outside, no record of what is visible from outside refutes Law 1. If the length of life and virginity are taken as visible from outside, the pair of Law 1 and Law 10 can be refuted.
ProofFirst half (𝒪 = {Obs}). Let M be a model that satisfies D6 and the nine laws other than Law 1, and let x be someone who is a virgin up to thirty and does not become a wizard while alive. Replace what lies inside x as follows. Let L(x) be the larger of 100 and the Λ of M. Make E(x), φ, the σ of the faces of x, N and κ the same as for the x of M₀, with φ and σ the same in every scene. Let Act(x) be {a_s : 21 ≤ s ≤ L(x)}, with each result r_s new, in neither Obs nor anyone else's results. Let Rel(x) be the set of those results, B(x, t) always true, and Toil(x, t) = t. Keep V(x), the other people, Obs, Wr and Harm as they are. Like the x of M₀, the replaced x is a wizard from time 21, satisfies L10 since L(x) ≥ Λ, and satisfies the other laws. Doing this for every such person gives a model, equal to M with respect to 𝒪, in which Law 1 holds. So no record of Obs separates a world where Law 1 holds from a world where it does not.
Second half (𝒪 = {Obs, L, V}). If there is even one person x with V(x, 30) true and L(x) = 30, then since Λ > 30, by L10 x is not a wizard. That contradicts L1. So this observation refutes the pair of Law 1 and Law 10. Q.E.D.
NoteFollowing the note to Law 10 in Book I, this version reads L as something not visible from outside. Read that way, the consequence of the second half is cut off. That consequence would be a claim about the body's lifespan, so this play, through the note, does not make it.

Proposition 12. Every face of a regular polyhedron has an opposite face if and only if the central inversion −I belongs to its symmetry group. Of the five, only the regular tetrahedron lacks this.
ProofSuppose −I belongs to the symmetry group G. −I sends faces to faces and sends the centre p of a face to −p, so the face centred at −p is the opposite of the face at p. Conversely, suppose every face has an opposite. Then the set S of face centres equals −S. The polyhedron is the intersection of the half-spaces {y : ⟨y, p⟩ ≤ ⟨p, p⟩}, one for each face centre p. An orthogonal map that sends S to itself sends this intersection to itself, and so belongs to G. −I sends S to itself, so it belongs to G. For the regular tetrahedron, by Proposition 6, −p is not the centre of any face. So its symmetry group T_d ≅ S₄ (order 24) does not contain −I. The group O_h ≅ S₄ × C₂ (order 48) of the regular hexahedron and the regular octahedron, and the group I_h ≅ A₅ × C₂ (order 120) of the regular dodecahedron and the regular icosahedron, contain −I. Q.E.D.

Proposition 13. Counting assignments that coincide under a rotation as the same, the number of ways to assign negative or positive to each face is 5 for the regular tetrahedron, 10 for the regular hexahedron, 23 for the regular octahedron, 96 for the regular dodecahedron, and 17,824 for the regular icosahedron.
ProofBy Burnside's lemma, the number equals the average (1/#G⁺) Σ_g 2^{c(g)} over the rotation group G⁺, where c(g) is the number of cycles that g makes on the faces. The orders of the rotation groups are 12, 24, 24, 60 and 60, in that order.
Regular tetrahedron: (2⁴ + 8·2² + 3·2²)/12 = 60/12 = 5.
Regular hexahedron: (2⁶ + 6·2³ + 3·2⁴ + 8·2² + 6·2³)/24 = 240/24 = 10.
Regular octahedron: (2⁸ + 6·2² + 3·2⁴ + 8·2⁴ + 6·2⁴)/24 = 552/24 = 23.
Regular dodecahedron: (2¹² + 24·2⁴ + 20·2⁴ + 15·2⁶)/60 = 5,760/60 = 96.
Regular icosahedron: (2²⁰ + 24·2⁴ + 20·2⁸ + 15·2¹⁰)/60 = 1,069,440/60 = 17,824. Q.E.D.

Book III. The System of RelationshipsLIBER III · DE SYSTEMATE RELATIONUM

Book III sets out the dynamics of relationships between people as polyhedra facing one another.
People are points and relationships are lines, and only the signs of the faces that meet at the two ends of a line are considered. No attribute of a person is placed anywhere.
Definitions 11 to 14 and the third and fourth postulates of this book were all added in this version.
The postulates of Book III lie outside the formulas of Book II. Propositions 8 to 11 of Book II claim nothing about a world with the postulates of Book III added. The thickness of lines is not counted in the Obs of Book II.

Definition 11. Interface. An interface is the pair made of the face that x turns toward y and the face that y turns toward x, at a time and in a scene. In each scene a person has one partner, and one of the elements of x turns a face toward that partner.
Originalなし（この版にて補ふ） (none; added in this version)

Definition 12. Sign of an interface. The sign of an interface is the product of the signs of its two faces. Positive with positive, and negative with negative, give positive; positive with negative gives negative.
Originalなし（この版にて補ふ） (none; added in this version)

Definition 13. Concord, bias, clash. An interface of positive and positive is called concord, one of positive and negative bias, and one of negative and negative clash. The sign of a clash is positive. It is called hidden concord.
Originalなし（この版にて補ふ） (none; added in this version)

Definition 14. Network of relationships. The network of relationships has people as points and relationships as lines. Each line has a thickness and a sign. The sign of a line is the sign of its interface.
Originalなし（この版にて補ふ） (none; added in this version)

Postulate 3. Force. Concord thickens a line by one, and bias thins it by one, but the thickness never goes below zero. Clash leaves the thickness as it is. In every interface, the accumulation of the side that shows a negative grows by one.

Postulate 4. Flow of return. A result of x thickens a line that has a neighbour of x at one end and does not have x at either end.

Clash is called "hidden concord" for two reasons.
First, the product of negative and negative is positive. Second, by Postulate 3, a clash adds to the accumulation at both ends. Since each scene has one partner, for someone with a single element this way of adding matches the count of the history H in Book II. By Law 4, for someone whose accumulation has reached the threshold, the opposite of a face once shown as negative appears as positive. If two people each turn toward the other the opposite of their former negative face, their interface becomes concord.

Proposition 14. Shape makes no difference. The rotation group of each of the five regular polyhedra acts transitively on its faces. So between elements of the same shape, what tells interfaces apart is not the position of the face but only the sign.
ProofBy the orbit-stabilizer theorem, the size of the orbit of a face equals the order of the group divided by the number of rotations that fix the face. The rotations that fix a face are the rotations about the axis through its centre, and their number equals the number of edges of the face. The order, the number of faces and the number of edges are: regular tetrahedron 12 = 4 × 3, regular hexahedron 24 = 6 × 4, regular octahedron 24 = 8 × 3, regular dodecahedron 60 = 12 × 5, regular icosahedron 60 = 20 × 3. So the orbit of a face is the whole set of faces, and every face can be carried to every other by a rotation. Two interfaces between elements of the same shape can have their facing faces brought into coincidence by rotation, so they carry no distinction by the position of the face. The only distinction left is the sign of Definition 13. For interfaces between elements of different shapes, too, Definition 13 tells them apart by sign alone. Q.E.D.
NoteNo one is superior in relationships because of the shape of their elements. This agrees with Postulate 2.

Proposition 15. For each of the five regular polyhedra, the number of vertices minus the number of edges plus the number of faces is two.
ProofCount the vertices, edges and faces. The number of edges is half the product of the number of faces and the number of edges of one face.
Regular tetrahedron: 4 − 6 + 4 = 2.
Regular hexahedron: 8 − 12 + 6 = 2.
Regular octahedron: 6 − 12 + 8 = 2.
Regular dodecahedron: 20 − 30 + 12 = 2.
Regular icosahedron: 12 − 30 + 20 = 2. Q.E.D.

Proposition 16. The regular hexahedron and the regular octahedron, and the regular dodecahedron and the regular icosahedron, are dual to each other: each pair swaps its numbers of faces and vertices, and shares its number of edges and its rotation group. The regular tetrahedron is its own dual.
ProofPlaced in dual position, the centre of each face of one points to a vertex of the other. So the numbers of faces and of vertices swap. Each edge corresponds to exactly one edge joining the centres of two adjacent faces, so the numbers of edges are equal. In dual position the two share the rotations that preserve the same set of points, so their rotation groups are equal. In other positions the groups are conjugate to each other. The face centres of the regular tetrahedron point in the directions −v, and these again form the vertices of a regular tetrahedron. Q.E.D.
NoteFor two people who are duals, a face of one meets a vertex of the other. In the words of the play, this is called a mirror relationship.

Proposition 17. A cycle in the network of relationships is said to be balanced when the product of the signs of its lines is positive. All cycles are balanced if and only if the people can be split into two groups so that lines within a group have positive sign and lines between the groups have negative sign. One of the groups may be empty.
ProofThe signs of the lines are read at one chosen time and scene. Sufficiency. Every cycle crosses the boundary between the groups an even number of times. So it has an even number of negative lines, and the product is positive. Necessity. In each connected component of the network, pick one person r. Give each person x the product of the signs of the lines on a path from r to x. Two paths together form a closed walk. A closed walk splits into cycles, apart from lines traversed twice, and lines traversed twice do not change the product. All cycles are balanced, so this product does not depend on the path. Split the people into those with a positive product and those with a negative one; then the sign of a line xy equals the product of the values given to x and to y. So lines within a group are positive and lines between the groups are negative. Joining the groups of each connected component gives two groups for the whole network. Q.E.D.
NoteThis proposition is the structure theorem for balance: the idea of balance in Heider (1946), set on networks by Harary (1953) and by Cartwright and Harary (1956). In Heider, a relationship of mutual dislike is negative. Here the sign of a clash is set as positive, and on that point this departs from Heider. Since a clash line has positive sign, two people who show each other negatives stand in the same group. The groups are a matter of shape, fixed by the signs of the lines; they do not depend on any attribute of a person.

Proposition 18. In a ring-shaped network of four or more people, seeing only one line known to have thickened through a result is not enough to pin its source on one person.
ProofBy Postulate 4, the source that thickens a line yz is someone next to y or z who is not an end of the line. In a ring of four or more, the neighbour of y other than z and the neighbour of z other than y are two different people. Taking either as the source fits Postulate 4. So from one line, two candidates for the source remain. Q.E.D.
NoteIn a ring of three, these two are the same person, and if the line is known to have thickened through a result, the source is settled. Lines also thicken through concord, so when that is not known, even three people do not settle it. Also, by Postulate 4, a result of x does not thicken a line with x at an end, so the lines of x do not thicken from the results of x.

Book IV. What Magic IsLIBER IV · DE MAGIA

The canon defines the wizard, but it does not define magic itself.
Book IV reads three things as pointing to one working: Definition 1 (an action using negative and positive together), Definition 8 (revealing the opposite as positive), and Law 6 (return to relationships, and being invisible from outside). It calls that working magic.
Definitions 15 and 16 of this book were added in this version.

Definition 15. Transmutation. Transmutation is using, later and as positive, the opposite of a face once shown as negative.
Originalなし（この版にて補ふ） (none; added in this version)

Definition 16. Magic. Magic is an action that produces a result by means of transmutation and returns that result to relationships between people that cannot be seen from outside.
Originalなし（この版にて補ふ） (none; added in this version)

In formulas, this goes as follows. An action a uses transmutation when Use(a) contains both a negative orientation (e, s, c) and an orientation (e, s′, c′) with s < s′, φ(e, s′, c′) = ι(φ(e, s, c)) and σ(ι(φ(e, s, c)), s′, c′) = +. The negative orientation that a transmutation points to is an event of the history H; whether it still remains in the accumulation N does not matter. The action a is magic when a uses transmutation, R(a) ≠ ∅, R(a) ⊆ Rel(x), and R(a) ∩ Obs = ∅.

Proposition 19. Where Law 6 holds, magic is nothing other than a transmutation that produces results.
ProofMagic has four essentials: transmutation, a result, return to relationships, and invisibility from outside. Law 6 states that every result lies within relationships and that relationships cannot be seen from outside. So a transmutation that produces results meets the remaining two essentials by itself, and is magic. The converse holds by definition. In the model M₆, which breaks Law 6, there is a transmutation whose result can be seen, and it is not magic. Q.E.D.
NoteThe root of magic lies in transmutation. Return and invisibility are supplied by Law 6, on the side of the world.

Proposition 20. One who does magic is a wizard. But a wizard does not necessarily do magic.
ProofFirst half. A transmutation uses a negative orientation and a positive orientation together. So an action that is magic is an action in the sense of Definition 1, and the one who does it is a wizard at that time. Second half. Let the model M″ be M₀ with the opposite f* turned as positive at time 21, f turned as positive from time 22, Act(x) = {a_s : 22 ≤ s ≤ 100} and Rel(x) = {r_s : 22 ≤ s ≤ 100}, where a_s uses the negative of f and the positive of f. M″ satisfies D6 and the ten laws, and x is a wizard from time 22, but no action uses transmutation, so x does no magic. Q.E.D.

Proposition 21. Magic exists only after the switch.
ProofLet a be a magic of x, with (e, s, c) the negative orientation of its transmutation and (e, s′, c′) the positive orientation. By the definition of action, e is an element of x and s < s′ ≤ τ(a) ≤ L(x). So (e, s, c) belongs to H(x, s′), and by Law 3 it remains in N(x, s′). Since s < s′ and its opposite is turned as positive at time s′, by Definition 8 the switch holds at time s′. And s′ ≤ τ(a). Q.E.D.
NoteWithout Law 3, negatives would vanish from the accumulation, and a later transmutation would be no proof of the switch. Let M₃′ be M₀ with N(x, t) always empty, plus a copy z of the x of M₀. M₃′ breaks only Law 3, and before the magic of x there is no switch at all. The link between magic and the switch stands on the negative never fading.

Proposition 22. Magic does not use up the negative. From a single negative, magic can be done every year for as long as one lives.
ProofWhat a transmutation points to is an event of the history, so using it does not reduce it. This does not depend on Law 3. In the model M₀, the only negative orientation is the one at time 20, and all eighty of the actions a_s (21 ≤ s ≤ 100) are magic that uses that one negative and the opposite positive at time s. M₀ satisfies the ten laws. Q.E.D.
NoteA negative is not fuel but a pattern. Since it is never used up, magic never runs out. Still, by Law 10, the toil grows with the length of the life lived.

Proposition 23. The results of magic cannot be seen from outside. Magic does not thicken the doer's own lines. In a ring-shaped network of four or more people, one line known to have thickened through a result does not settle its source.
ProofThat the results cannot be seen from outside follows from Definition 16. By Postulate 4, a result thickens lines that do not have the doer at an end, so the doer's own lines do not thicken from that magic. The last part follows from Proposition 18. Q.E.D.

Conclusion. Magic is turning a negative into the positive on its opposite side and returning it to relationships no one can see. The negative is never used up, the results cannot be seen, and the doer's own lines do not thicken from that magic. In a ring of four or more, the source is not settled by one line.

Book V. The Ban on MagicLIBER V · DE INTERDICTO MAGIAE

Book V takes the operator's intuition as its skeleton. Its text is placed, without a single character changed, as the original of Definition 20.
The magic of Book IV was a working on relationships. Magic here is the way a theory is placed. The word is the same, but it points to something else, so this is called the second sense of magic.
Definitions 17 to 19 and the fifth postulate were added in this version.

Definition 20. Magic (second sense). Magic is a heterodox theory placed in the immune position. Heterodox means not accepted within knowledge. The device of placing a heterodox theory in the immune position is called the art of magic.

Postulate 5. Knowledge. A theory accepted within knowledge must be refutable.

Postulate 5, like Postulate 2, is a norm, and it is placed outside the system of formulas that state facts. Popper drew the line between empirical science and what is not science at whether a theory can be refuted. Here that line is moved to become the line between knowledge and belief. Popper also named the moves of adding auxiliary hypotheses, revising definitions, or doubting observations in order to escape refutation conventionalist stratagems, and ruled them out as rules of method. The art of magic is such a stratagem. Knowledge is taken to be everything that follows from the whole of the accepted theories.

Proposition 24. If observation is limited to Obs and virginity, the Principia is in the immune position.
ProofLet 𝒪 = {Obs, V}. For any record, take the following model. The set of people is as in the record, and everyone has the form of the x of M₀. Virginity is as in the record, and L is the larger of 100 and Λ. The names of results are new and do not overlap Obs. This model is a model of the world, satisfies D6 and the ten laws, and realizes the record. So the Principia is in the immune position. Q.E.D.
Noteverification/models.js checks this by realizing 112 records: one to three people, every combination of time-independent virginity, and several choices of Obs and Λ.

Proposition 25. The note to Law 10 that this version added is an art of magic.
ProofIf 𝒪 = {Obs, V, L}, a record in which some person x has V(x, 30) true and L(x) = 30 can be realized in a model of the world but not in a model of the Principia. If x is made a wizard, L10 is broken (Λ > 30), and if x is not, L1 is broken. So under this observation the Principia is refutable. The note to Law 10 separates the sense of "living long" from the body's lifespan, and through it this version removes L from the observation. Then by Proposition 24, the Principia moves into the immune position. The Principia is not accepted within knowledge. Every page of this site declares in its top band that it is a space separate from the operator's academic work, and that it is not science. So the Principia is heterodox. So this note is a device of placing a heterodox theory in the immune position, that is, an art of magic. Q.E.D.
NoteLaw 6 (results cannot be seen from outside) and the advice of Law 9 (write no specifics; not put into the formulas) are also rules that cut off the means of checking. The canon already holds, within itself, a mechanism of immunity.

Proposition 26. If acceptance into knowledge is decided by records alone, no theory in the immune position may be accepted. Accepting one would mean accepting two contradictory theories together, and knowledge would become inconsistent.
ProofLet 𝒪 = {Obs, V}. Lemma. Every theory in the immune position realizes all the records that are realized in models of the world. So the theories in the immune position all realize exactly the same records. If acceptance is decided by records alone, then either every theory in the immune position is accepted or none is. Let T⁺ be the Principia with "there is a wizard" added, and T⁻ the Principia with "there is no wizard" added. T⁺ realizes every record, by the construction of Proposition 24. T⁻ realizes every record by the following construction. Everyone has the form of M₀⁻ (a switch, but no actions), Obs and virginity are as in the record, virgins have an L of at least twenty-one and below thirty (for example 25), and everyone else has L = 100. Since L is at least twenty-one, everyone has the switch at time 21. So both T⁺ and T⁻ are in the immune position. If a theory in the immune position were accepted, T⁺ and T⁻ would both be accepted, and knowledge would contain "there is a wizard" and "there is no wizard" at once, and become inconsistent. So none may be accepted. Q.E.D.
NoteRefutable theories can be told apart by records. With 𝒪 = {Obs, V, L}, a record with V(x, 30) true and L(x) = 50 is realized in a model of T⁺ (Λ = 40) but in no model of T⁻ (since L1 demands a wizard). Theories in the immune position cannot be told apart by any record. That is where the two kinds differ.

Corollary. Magic must not be within knowledge.
By Postulate 5, a theory in the immune position is not accepted within knowledge (Proposition 26 shows why). Magic is a heterodox theory placed in the immune position, so it must not be within knowledge. That magic is absent is not a fact about the world, but a ban that keeps knowledge consistent.

NotePostulate 5 and Proposition 26 bind only what lies within knowledge. Being outside knowledge goes against neither. Every page of this site declares in its top band "This is an entertainment space, separate from the operator's academic work. It is not science." and does not seek to enter knowledge. The Principia exists as magic within the frame of the play, which is one place outside knowledge.

Conclusion. Magic cannot be within knowledge not because it does not exist, but because it must not be there. The place where it must not be is within knowledge; the place where it may be is outside knowledge. This play declares, in the band at the top of every page, that it stands outside knowledge.

General ScholiumSCHOLIUM GENERALE

  • Readings used in moving to formulas. Law 1 was read as "while alive". Law 2 was read as "every element of every person", and Law 5 as "someone". If Law 2 were read as "some element of some person", it would follow from Law 5 together with Definition 8 and Definition 10, and would no longer be independent. That is because a switch is an element turning, after a face f, the opposite of f, and by Definition 10 the opposite of f differs from f.
  • Other readings. Definition 6 was read as "every face is negative at some time and scene, and positive at some time and scene". Definition 1 was read as "one action uses negative and positive together", and once someone has become a wizard, they stay one. The switch was taken to hold at every time when the opposite is turned as positive (only whether it occurs is used). Law 4 was read as the switch coming after the time when the threshold is first reached. Law 6 was read as "results lie within relationships, and relationships cannot be seen from outside"; "at the very moment it appears" is not put into the formulas. The "never stop" of Law 7 was read as there being at least one result-producing action each year. "So the specifics are not written" in Law 9 is advice drawn from harm coming back, and is not put into the formulas. Law 10 was read in one direction only: "to become one is to live long" as living at least Λ once one has become one, with "hard" read as the toil growing every year. Postulate 1 was folded into taking time as ℕ and into how the history H is defined. Law 8 was read as the brain working in every year after becoming one. "Their own" in Definition 1 was read as every orientation that the action uses belonging to the elements of x. The time of an action was taken to lie within the length of life. The times and scenes of Definition 6 are not limited to the length of life.
  • Postulate 2 is a postulate about the worth of persons, and it is placed outside the system of formulas that state facts. Following the view (Hume) that no conclusion about worth follows from premises that state facts alone, worth is not proved by formulas but set down first, as a postulate.
  • The bound for long life Λ and what is visible from outside, Obs, are fixed not by the laws but by the model. However Λ > 30 and Obs are fixed, Propositions 8 to 11 hold once the length of life and the names of results are chosen again (in M₀, L(x) ≥ Λ; in M₁₀, 30 ≤ L(x) < Λ; and except in M₆, results are taken outside Obs).
  • The name "General Scholium" follows the Scholium Generale that the second edition (1713) of Newton's Mathematical Principles of Natural Philosophy added at its end.
  • Postulate 3 and Postulate 4 of Book III set out the dynamics of relationships as a game; they do not measure people's relationships. Propositions 14 to 18 follow from those rules, the shapes of the regular polyhedra, and the shape of the network.
  • Definition 15 and Definition 16 of Book IV read Definition 1, Definition 8 and Law 6 of the canon as one working; they are not in the canon. Transmutation points not to the accumulation N of the "accumulated negative" in Definition 8 but to the negatives of the history H. That is why magic coming after the switch depends on Law 3. Propositions 19 to 23 follow from those definitions, the ten laws, and Postulate 4 of Book III.
  • Definitions 17 to 19 and the fifth postulate of Book V were added in this version to move the operator's intuition (the original of Definition 20) into the language of models and observation; they are not in the canon. Definition 20 reads the "what is placed" of the intuition as the theory that is placed, and separates the device of placing as "the art of magic". How the observation 𝒪 is taken is stated for each proposition.
