# Playground: The World a Wizard Sees

> This is a play. What moves here turns the definitions and laws of the canon into a game of pictures and numbers. It measures nothing, predicts nothing, and records nothing.

The world a wizard sees probably looks something like this.
From outside, there is just a person standing with an umbrella.
In the wizard's own eyes, though, faces turn, negatives pile up, one day their opposites begin to glow, and each result melts away into relationships.
The five figures below walk through it in order, and then the playground lets it run.

## In figures

### 1. Elements are regular polyhedra, and the face they show depends on the time and the circumstances

![Figure 1 An element, and the face it shows depending on the time and the circumstances (a play)](fig:fig-faces)

The elements that make up a person are regular polyhedra, like dice (Definition 5).
The same element turns a different face outward when the scene changes (Law 2).
A face is neither negative nor positive in itself; the scene decides (Definition 6).

### 2. Negatives do not fade; they pile up

![Figure 2 The store of negatives never shrinks (a play)](fig:fig-stock)

Each time a negative face is shown, one more goes into the jar.
The jar never empties (Law 3).
How full it must get before the next step differs from person to person.

### 3. The switch: the opposite of a negative glows as positive

![Figure 3 The switch: the opposite of a negative face appears as positive (a play)](fig:fig-switch)

Once the jar is full, the negative face stays as it is, and the face across from it begins to glow as positive (Law 4).
Thirty is only a rule of thumb for this, and it can come earlier (Law 5). The playground is built so that it can also come later.
A regular tetrahedron has no opposite face, so the opposite vertex glows instead ([Proposition 6](strict.en.html#prop-6) of the [Principia](strict.en.html)).

### 4. A result melts into relationships the moment it appears

![Figure 4 Results return to relationships and cannot be seen from outside (a play)](fig:fig-return)

A result produced by a wizard melts into the relationships with the people around, as if nothing had happened (Law 6).
Someone outside sees nothing.

### 5. Two views

![Figure 5 The wizard's view and the outside view (a play)](fig:fig-two-views)

On the left is the world the wizard sees. On the right is the world seen from outside.
Both show the same person.

## Play: laws at work on relationships

The laws of the canon work on the relationships between people.
The playground below draws only the 8 people and the lines of the relationships between them.
The people carry no names, ages, genders, or any other attributes. Only the changes in relationships are shown.

1. **Change the scene**: the face the wizard shows each person (negative or positive) is chosen again.
2. **Live one day**: negatives pile up, one for each person shown a negative face.
3. **Watch for the switch**: when the store reaches the threshold, the opposite of each line where a negative was shown glows as positive. From then on, each day produces results, and the relationship between each partner and their neighbour grows thicker. The neighbour is always someone other than the wizard.
4. **Let the days run**: the days pass on their own, and now and then the scene changes too.
5. **Compare the two views**: the outside view also shows lines growing thicker. But every dot is drawn the same, and one thickened line does not tell whose doing it was.

The wizard's own lines do not grow thicker from the wizard's own results ([Proposition 23](strict.en.html#prop-23) of the [Principia](strict.en.html)).
Watching the outside view for a long time, one may notice the lines that stay thin.
Even so, one thickened line does not settle where it came from ([Proposition 18](strict.en.html#prop-18)).

## The laws and how they work in the playground

| Law | How it works in the playground |
| --- | --- |
| Law 2 Many Faces | "Change the scene" can turn the face shown to the same person from negative to positive or back |
| Law 3 Conservation | The store only grows from day to day; it never shrinks |
| Law 4 Reversal | When the store reaches the threshold, the lines to those shown a negative glow gold |
| Law 5 Rule of Thumb | The threshold is a count of the store, not an age. Each time the page opens it is set somewhere between 18 and 36. Taking the rule-of-thumb age of thirty as the number thirty, a lower threshold means an early switch and a higher one a late switch |
| Law 6 Return | A result thickens the relationship between the partner and their neighbour. The wizard's own lines do not thicken, and the visible results stay at 0 |
| Law 7 Common Practice | In the outside view, the wizard is drawn as the same dot as the other 7 people. Whoever has become one, the same things happen |
| Law 8 Quagmire | After the switch, pressing "Stop" stops only the screen |
| Law 9 Secrecy | What the results are is written nowhere. Only lines growing thicker are shown |
| Law 10 Long Life | The days keep going until they are stopped |

## House rules

- No attributes are given to people. No one is represented.
- This playground runs only inside the browser. Nothing pressed is sent anywhere or kept. Closing the page clears the store as well. That differs from Law 3 of the canon, but it is a choice made on the playground's side.
- In browsers set to reduce motion, the glowing movement is left out.
